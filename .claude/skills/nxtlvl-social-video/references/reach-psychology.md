# Reach and hook psychology (read before writing any script)

Researched 2026-11-23 after two Creedom reels flatlined (304 and 270 views, 5 s and 4 s average watch on 37.5 s and
23.5 s reels). Each claim is tagged: **[IG]** Instagram or Meta said it, **[R]** peer-reviewed research, **[V]**
vendor or blog data (directional only, conflicting numbers). Sources at the end.

## 1. What Instagram actually rewards

1. **Three signals decide reach: watch time, likes per reach, sends per reach.** [IG, Mosseri, Jan 2025, repeated
   through 2026] Likes matter slightly more with followers ("connected" reach), sends (DM shares) matter slightly more
   with non-followers ("unconnected" reach). So: hold attention to grow reach at all, and give people a reason to send
   it to one friend to break out of your followers.
2. **The first 3 seconds are measured on their own.** [IG, Aug 2025] Insights replaced "view rate" with **skip rate**:
   the share of views lost in the first 3 s. The retention chart shows the rest. Diagnose with both:
   high skip rate = the hook failed; low skip rate but a steep drop from 3 s to 10 s = the hook promised something the
   next seconds did not pay off. [V] Healthy skip rate is somewhere around 20-35 %; above 40-50 % reach usually stalls
   (two small-account case studies, correlational).
3. **Distribution is staged.** [IG/V] A reel goes to a small test group first; only if watch time and sends hold does it
   go wider. A curve that jumps on day 1 and then goes flat (both Creedom reels) means it failed that first test.
4. **Trial Reels** [IG, Dec 2024; public accounts with 1,000+ followers from Jul 2025 per [V]]: a reel shown to
   non-followers only, ranked separately, with no effect on normal reels. Use it to A/B test hooks: post 2-3 hook
   variants of the same reel as trials, keep the winner, share it to everyone.
5. **Originality is rewarded, reposts and aggregator content are down-ranked** for non-follower reach [IG/V]. Our own
   footage, our own data and our own UI beat stock and reposted clips.
6. **Length:** reels up to 3 minutes can be recommended [V], but watch time is relative: a 15 s reel watched 12 s beats
   a 40 s reel watched 6 s. Make it as short as the payoff allows. Loops (an ending that cuts back into the start) add
   watch time.

## 2. Why people stop, stay and share (the psychology)

1. **Curiosity is an information gap.** [R, Loewenstein 1994; Golman and Loewenstein] People feel a pull to close a gap
   between what they know and what they want to know, and making a question *salient* raises that pull. A small "bite"
   of information increases the hunger for the rest. So: open a specific question in second 1 and close it late; never
   answer it in the hook. Too vague a gap does nothing: the viewer must already know enough to care.
2. **Self-relevance wins attention.** People notice what is about *them* (their reel, their money, their mistake).
   "This AI told me..." is about a tool; "Your reel dies at second 3" is about the viewer.
3. **Negativity and stakes beat neutral.** Losses, mistakes and threats get attention faster than gains. [V] Tension and
   outrage openers out-view inspirational ones by a wide margin in large TikTok samples; stakes-escalation hooks lead
   completion on Shorts. Use mistakes, flops, "you're losing X", not "tips for X".
4. **Specific beats general.** [V] Concrete numbers, names and timestamps ("304 views", "0:03", "62 %") read as real and
   pass the "is this an ad?" test. Round claims and adjectives read as marketing.
5. **Pattern interrupt and mid-action starts.** [V] Openings that start in the middle of the action (no setup, no logo,
   no "today I'll show you") hold better than sequential stories. Frame 1 must already be moving and already show the
   subject.
6. **Ads trigger avoidance.** Viewers scroll the moment a reel looks or sounds like a pitch: a brand card or mascot on
   frame 1, a product name in the first sentence, "there's an app that...". Earn the right to name the product: problem,
   proof, then the tool as the answer (around two thirds in).
7. **Proof and authenticity.** Real screens, real numbers, a real face (even 2 s) and imperfect native-looking text beat
   polished animation that could be anything. Animation is the wrapper, real proof is the content.
8. **Why people share** [R, Berger, STEPPS and the arousal study]: **social currency** (sharing makes me look smart or
   in the know), **triggers**, **high-arousal emotion** (amusement, surprise, anxiety, anger beat calm or sad), **public**,
   **practical value** (useful to someone I know) and **stories**. Sends happen when the viewer thinks of one specific
   person: write that person into the reel ("send this to the friend whose reels keep flopping").
9. **Irony and self-deprecation** are high-arousal and socially safe: a creator showing their own flop is relatable,
   specific and shareable.

## 3. Hook formulas that fit these rules

Every hook: on screen by frame 1, about the viewer or a specific result, a number or concrete detail, an open question,
no product name, no logo. The on-screen hook text and the spoken hook should add to each other, not repeat word for word.

| Formula | Pattern | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Own-flop post-mortem | Real bad number + irony | "This reel got 304 views. It was about why people stop watching." |
| Exact-moment stakes | You + a timestamp + a loss | "Your reel loses 62 % of people at 0:03. Here's the second they leave." |
| Contrarian | Kill a belief in one line | "Your hook isn't the problem. Your second 3 is." |
| Result-first story | Ending first, then how | "This app gave my reel 2.9 out of 5. It was right." |
| Test or challenge | Viewer plays along | "Rate this hook 1 to 5. Then see what the AI said." |
| Mistake list | Number + mistake + you | "3 things that kill your reel before anyone sees it." |
| Before / after | Two states, gap between | "Same reel. 270 views vs [X]. One change." |

Avoid as openers: "This AI...", "There's an app that...", "Here are X tips", "Did you know", any greeting, any logo.

## 4. Script rules (apply with the story arc in SKILL.md § 3a)

1. Hook at 70 (0-2 s): formula from § 3, visual proof on frame 1.
2. First peak by 3-6 s: the twist, the irony, the shocking number.
3. Name the product no earlier than about 60 % of the runtime, as the answer to the problem already shown.
4. A visual change every 1-2 s; burned-in captions; no dead air.
5. 12-25 s for a single idea; cut every line that does not raise or pay off the question.
6. One share trigger (a specific person to send it to) and one save trigger (a list or steps worth keeping).
7. CTA last: the keyword line (house rule), straight after the final peak; end abruptly or loop to frame 1.
8. Every number must be real (the owner's insights, the app's real output). If we don't have it yet, write a
   [PLACEHOLDER] and ask; never invent a stat.
9. Write 2-3 hook variants for every reel so the owner can test them as Trial Reels.
10. After posting, read skip rate and the retention chart: high skip = change the hook; drop at 3-10 s = fix the payoff.
