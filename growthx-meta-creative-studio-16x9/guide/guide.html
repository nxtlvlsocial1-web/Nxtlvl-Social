What Ads Creative Studio does

Ads Creative Studio is Meta's end-to-end creative workspace for advertisers. Instead of guessing what to make next, it reads your ad performance, tells you what's working, uses Meta's Gen AI to create new ad concepts in your brand style, and keeps improving them as results come in.

One self-improving loop

🔍InsightsWhat's working in your best ads
✨CreateGen AI concepts on brand
🧪TestComment, iterate, A/B test
📈LearnOptimise and scale winners

What makes it different

  • Benchmarks: it compares your ads with top performers in your niche, not just with your own past ads.
  • Specific suggestions: it tells you what to change, such as the hook, the visual or the offer.
  • Brand-aware AI: new concepts follow your messaging, branding and creative guidelines.
  • You stay in control: nothing publishes without your approval.

Rolling out gradually. Meta is releasing Ads Creative Studio to advertisers in stages, so it may not be in your account yet, and menu names can differ slightly from this guide. Look for it inside Ads Manager / Meta Business Suite, and use the prep on the next page in the meantime: it makes every Meta campaign better anyway.

BEFORE YOU START

Prepare your account and your brand

The tool is only as good as the data and brand inputs you give it. Tick these off first.

📊 Account and data

  • You have admin or advertiser access to the ad account in Meta Business Suite.
  • The Meta Pixel (and ideally the Conversions API) is installed and tracking purchases or leads.
  • You have run ads recently, so there is real performance data for the insights to read.
  • Your campaigns optimise for the result you actually want (purchases or leads, not just clicks).

🎨 Brand kit

  • Logo files, brand colours and fonts.
  • Clean product photos and 3–5 of your best existing ads or videos.
  • A one-paragraph brand voice: who you sell to, how you talk (for example "simple, bold, Hinglish"), and words you never use.
  • Your key messages: top 3 benefits, proof (reviews, results), current offer and price.

📋 Creative guidelines (your do's and don'ts)

  • Claims you can and can't make (for example no "guaranteed results" or medical claims).
  • Must-haves: logo placement, disclaimer text, product shown in the first seconds.
  • Styles that fit your brand (UGC, studio, lifestyle) and styles to avoid.

Tip: write all of this in one simple document. You'll paste from it when you set up the brand inputs in Step 2.

STEP 1

Read the insights: what's actually working

Start in the insights view. Your goal here is a short list of things to keep and things to fix.

1

Find your best performers. Sort by your main result (cost per purchase or lead, or ROAS), not by likes or clicks.

2

Spot the pattern. What do the winners share: the hook, the format (UGC, static, carousel), the offer, the length, the first frame?

3

Compare with the niche benchmark. Where your ads fall behind the top performers in your category is where the quickest gains are.

4

Turn suggestions into a test list. Write each suggestion as one clear change you can test.

STEP 2

Generate new ad concepts with Gen AI

Meta's Gen AI turns your insights into new ad concepts. Give it your brand inputs first, then guide it like you would brief a designer.

Set up your brand inputs

  • Messaging: your audience, top benefits, proof and offer (from your brand document).
  • Branding: logo, colours, fonts and product images.
  • Guidelines: your do's and don'ts, banned claims and required disclaimers.

Example guidance you can give it

Make 3 concepts for [product] aimed at [audience]. Open with the result in the first 2 seconds, show the product in use, and end with [offer]. Keep the tone simple and bold, in Hinglish.
Take our best ad ([ad name]) and make a version for [new product], keeping the same hook and structure.
Make this concept feel more premium: cleaner background, fewer words on screen, close-up product shots.

Review every concept before it goes further

  • Every claim is true and allowed for your category.
  • Product, price and offer are correct.
  • It looks and sounds like your brand.
  • The first frame would stop you scrolling.

STEP 3

Iterate, then A/B test

Use Comment, Guide and Iterate to refine a concept until you'd happily run it, then launch a test straight from the studio.

Test rules that keep results honest

1

One variable per test. Change only the hook, or only the visual, or only the offer, so you know what made the difference.

2

Same audience and budget split. Give each version a fair, equal chance.

3

Give it time. Let a test run for about a week, or until each version has a meaningful number of results, before you judge it.

4

Judge on your main result. Cost per purchase or lead (or ROAS) decides the winner, not likes.

Let it keep optimising

As performance data comes in, the studio keeps honing the creative. Check it weekly: keep what's improving, pause what isn't, and feed new learnings back into your guidance.

You stay in control: nothing publishes without your approval, so review each new version before it goes live.

STEP 4

Scale your winners

Once a concept clearly beats the others on your main result, put more behind it and multiply it.

Do thisHow
Raise the budget graduallyIncrease in steady steps rather than doubling overnight, and watch cost per result as you go.
New stylesTurn the winning static into a short video or carousel with the same hook and message.
New productsApply the winning structure to your other products.
Fresh hooksKeep the body, test 3 new openings to fight ad fatigue.

Your weekly 30-minute routine

  • Monday: read insights, update your test list.
  • Tuesday: generate and review 3–5 new concepts.
  • Wednesday: approve and launch one A/B test.
  • Friday: check results, pause losers, scale or vary winners.

The loop in one line: insights → create → test → learn → repeat. Every week your ads get a little smarter.
