In my town, every house keeps a jar of rice by the front door.
Because every night at midnight, the Counter lets himself in.
He's so tall, he has to fold in half to fit through the door.
His head is a big white cube, like a dice. And every black dot is an eye.
He can't take anyone until he's counted everything in the house. Every spoon. Every sock. Every single grain of rice.
The rice keeps him busy until sunrise. And at sunrise, he has to leave.
And if anything makes him lose count, he has to start again, from one.
I'm eleven, and keeping our jar full is my job.
Grandpa lives with us. He hates mess more than anything.
He vacuums after breakfast, after lunch, and once, at a wedding.
And when he sleeps, he snores like a lawnmower.
Last Tuesday, Mom had the night shift at the hospital.
Jar by the door, she said. Lights out by eleven.
I filled it right to the top, with the last bag of rice in the house.
At eleven fifty-five, our dog Biscuit chased a moth, straight into it.
Rice. Everywhere.
Grandpa didn't say a word. He just plugged in the vacuum.
By the time I got back with the broom, every grain was gone.
And Grandpa was asleep in his armchair, smiling.
Then the clock in the hall began to chime.
Twelve.
The front door creaked open.
One long, pale hand. Then another.
Then the Counter unfolded into our living room.
I dove behind the couch.
He looked at the empty jar. Then he started counting.
One lamp. Two cushions. Forty-one socks. Three hundred books.
Fast. Way too fast. And he was getting closer to Grandpa.
Then I saw the vacuum.
I ripped off the lid, yanked out the bag, and threw it as high as I could.
Rice and dust rained down over the whole room.
The Counter froze. All six eyes went wide.
One. Two. Three.
He got all the way to nine hundred.
Then Grandpa snored.
The Counter flinched. He'd lost count.
One. Two. Three.
Every time he got close, Grandpa snored again.
All. Night. Long.
When the sun hit the window, the Counter stood up, stared at me with all six eyes, and folded himself back out the door.
Grandpa woke up, looked at the floor, and sighed.
Who made this mess?
